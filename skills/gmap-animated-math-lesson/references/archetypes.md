# Deterministic lesson archetypes

Choose the smallest archetype that exposes the mathematical reasoning. The renderer may look polished, but the mathematical state must remain deterministic.

## Bar model

Use for ratios, fractions, comparison, part-whole, and multi-step word problems. Give every cell an ID, label the unit value, bracket the whole, and transform the picture into an equation only after the grouping is visible.

## Geometry angle

Use semantic points, segments, rays, angle arcs, and equal or parallel marks. Draw given conditions first, highlight one local relationship per beat, and preserve exact geometry independently of decorative hand-drawn styling.

## Shape count

Partition the answer into named non-overlapping groups. Count each group with a different stable cue, then show the sum. Validate that group counts equal the verified total and that no shape belongs to two groups unless inclusion-exclusion is explicitly taught.

## Algebra balance

Represent both sides as matched objects. Every algebraic transformation must occur on both sides in the same beat. Connect the visual change to the corresponding symbolic line.

## Number line

Give ticks, intervals, points, and jumps separate IDs. Animate direction and distance, not only the final point. Keep scale and tick spacing consistent.

## Coordinate plane

Treat axes, grid, points, segments, slopes, and regions as semantic objects. Narrate ordered pairs in axis order and highlight horizontal and vertical changes before stating slope.

## Table pattern

Highlight the specific row, column, or diagonal being compared. Transform observations into a rule only after at least two cases are visible. Keep input and output objects distinct.

## Combining archetypes

Combine only when one representation hands reasoning to another, such as bar model to equation or coordinate plane to table. Use an explicit `transform` beat and retain both representations in the final overview when that comparison is instructional.
