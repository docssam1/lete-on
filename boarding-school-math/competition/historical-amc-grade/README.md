# Historical AMC grade-level results — source staging

This folder contains historical, source-attributed aggregate data. The MAA-report grade means are connected to the AMC paper review as a narrowly matched historical reference: the paper year, contest, and an exact grade must all match. The reference appears only after every answer has been marked. It is labeled as a past cohort mean, never as a current norm, student level, percentile, or placement result. Korea-only score distributions remain research data and are not mixed into the national historical means.

`amc8-2011-korea-grade-percentile.csv` transcribes the MAA report table reproduced by AMC Study for the 2011 AMC 8 Korea participant cohort (899 reported participants). It contains only score-by-grade-group counts and the table's reported percentile values; no names, school records, or individual results are copied.

For a completed 2011 AMC 8 attempt, the paper-review screen can show a matching row from this table (grades 6–8 exact; grades 1–5 map to the report's combined `<=5` bucket). It displays the source's percentile value as historical Korea-cohort context, labels the cohort and bucket, and links back to the transcription. Rows without a reported percentile remain hidden. For a completed 2009 or 2010 AMC 8 attempt, it can calculate the share of the same-grade Korea cohort scoring strictly below the entered score from the complete score-frequency tables in `amc8-2009-korea-grade-score-count.csv` and `amc8-2010-korea-grade-score-counts.csv`. The 2010 table was transcribed from the full HTML text of an Internet Archive copy of MAA’s original Korea report; it contains 751 total participants and 742 with a listed Grade 5–8. The app displays the grade-specific denominator and labels the result as a historical cohort comparison. The screen labels that calculated method and links to its source; it does not call it an MAA-reported percentile. Neither comparison claims a current percentile, rank, placement, award, or US/international norm. `amc8-korea-percentile-reference.js` provides the screen lookup while the CSVs remain the transcription record of truth.

`amc8-korea-grade-means-2009-2011.csv` records the grade-level mean and participant counts printed in the 2009 and 2011 Korea AMC 8 result pages. These are Korea-reported cohorts, not US or global norms, and remain separate from MAA report-cohort averages. The 2009 listed grade counts total 737 of 741; the 2011 listed grade groups total 894 of 899. Keep those unaccounted-for grade records visible when using the table.

`amc8-2009-korea-grade-score-count.csv` transcribes the score-by-grade frequency table reproduced for the 2009 AMC 8 Korea participant cohort (741 reported participants). It has grade buckets 5–8 only and no percentile values. The four grade totals sum to 737, leaving four participants with no grade recorded in the report summary.

`amc8-2019-global-grade-score-counts.csv` records the complete score 0–25 frequency table for the 2019 AMC 8 MAA Overall grade-report image reproduced in a Beijing XDF results article. Apple Vision OCR was checked against both images. The file includes the report's unknown-grade group so all five group totals sum to 94,620. Weighted means round to the summary's reported values: ≤5, 8.48 (n=4,396); Grade 6, 8.50 (n=18,245); Grade 7, 9.35 (n=30,327); Grade 8, 10.06 (n=40,167); unknown grade, 8.09 (n=1,485). The ≤5 bucket is retained as such; it is not split into individual grades, and the unknown-grade group is not used for learner comparison. The review screen derives a strictly-lower-score share, not an official percentile, present-day norm, or ability band. The article reports that its images are MAA's 2019 score reports; the image host is a secondary reproduction, not MAA.

`amc8-2010-item-response-percent.csv` records the 2010 AMC 8 answer-choice percentages, omissions, unreadable responses, and correct-response rates from a scanned table attributed to MAA and reproduced by AMC Study. The post reports 153,211 students. The image was transcribed with Apple Vision and visually checked against the scan; correct answers were cross-checked against the LIVE answer key. The same file now includes the complete 2009 and 2011 answer-choice tables from AMC Study's MAA-result reproductions: 741 and 899 reported South Korea participants, respectively. For 2009 question 12, LIVE lists D as the answer while the reproduced response table marks D and E as correct; the app keeps D as the answer key and records their combined response rate (65.18%). These are item-response references for their recorded cohorts, not grade-level data, current norms, or student ability ratings. AMC Study's red prediction marks and self-reported prediction-hit claims are excluded. They appear only beside missed/unanswered items for matching AMC 8 years. The source host is a secondary reproduction, not the MAA host.

`amc8-award-cutoffs-2003-2022.csv` holds published AMC 8 whole-cohort Honor Roll (top 5%) and Distinguished Honor Roll (top 1%) minimum scores from the historical-results table for 2003–2020 and 2022. The paper-review screen shows the same-year reference after a complete manual score entry. This is a whole-cohort award cutoff, not a grade-level percentile or actual award decision. No 2021 row is added because there was no 2021 AMC 8 contest; 2023 onward uses the existing MAA EduView score-distribution reference. The old table is a secondary transcription attributed to MAA historical reports; cutoff semantics are preserved as the listed minimum score. Cross-checks include the Think Matrix table for 2013–2023 and the archived 2018 contest score report. The 2012/2013 boundary can appear as `>17` in one source, equivalent to a minimum qualifying score of 18; the staged file uses 18.

`amc8-grade-means-2001-2009.csv` stages recorded-grade participant counts and mean scores for AMC 8 years 2001–2009. The 2001–2002 cells come from an MAA-authored 2008 results report; the 2003–2009 cells come from an MAA-authored 2009 report. Both copies are hosted by third parties. Grade groups are 4–5, 6, 7, and 8. `amc8-grade-means-2010.csv` adds the 2010 MAA statistics page preserved from its original host by Internet Archive: exact grades 5–8, 150,616 recorded-grade participants; the page reports 153,211 participants overall, leaving 2,595 outside the grade rows. This is a grade-mean comparison only, not a score-by-grade percentile table.

`amc8-overall-cohort-summary-2005-2020.csv` records whole-cohort annual summaries: 2005–2019 from 99CEF's 2019 AMC 8 analysis report, and 2020 U.S. average (10.01) reported by the MAA Wisconsin Section. The 99CEF report labels 2005–2015 as Global and 2016–2019 as United States and Canada; the 2020 Wisconsin report gives a U.S. average only. These populations stay explicit and must not be blended into a single trend. 2020 participant count, perfect-score count, and score-frequency totals were not present in the recovered report, so those fields stay blank. The 2016 row preserves 99CEF's U.S./Canada count of 108,227; the Michigan MAA Section separately reported 108,295, so the source-specific discrepancy remains unresolved. This is whole-cohort context, not a grade-specific mean, percentile, award rule, or ability level. `amc8-overall-cohort-reference.js` shows an exact-year average comparison after the paper is complete, while the separate same-grade reference continues to show a grade mean only when an exact-grade table is available. 99CEF is the official Taiwan AMC administrator, but its PDF is not MAA-hosted. The 2020 mean comes from an MAA Section newsletter and has no cohort denominator in that source.

`amc-score-distribution-reference.js` also includes the MAA-hosted 2011 AMC 8 Contest Score Report preserved from its original URL in the Internet Archive. Apple Vision OCR was used as a transcription aid and the report image was visually checked. It reports 152,770 students, 2,411 schools, mean 10.76, standard deviation 4.44, and score cutoffs at the 1st, 5th, 25th, 50th, and 75th percentiles. This is an all-participant summary, not a grade-level norm. Its reported count differs from the 99CEF 2019 retrospective table's 2011 count (152,745); keep both as their respective source artifacts and do not reconcile them by assumption. The MAA image does not contain grade means, so it does not fill the Grade 7 comparison gap.

`amc10-12-grade-means-2007-2011.csv` stages 110 historical AMC 10/12 records from MAA-authored national statistical reports: grade-group mean and participant count, separated by contest, year, and A/B form. The 2007–2010 values are from the 2010 annual report; 2011 values are from a copy of the posted grand-statistics report. Grade groups preserve the report wording (`8 and below` vs exact `8`, and `unknown`). These are historical descriptive means, not percentile ranks, cut scores, or level labels.

`amc8-2010-item-concepts.csv` transcribes the question-level concept tags and difficulty ratings published alongside the MAA-authorized 2010 AMC 8 solutions on LIVE by Po-Shen Loh. All 25 questions are represented with a direct problem-source URL. The 2010 paper's GFIELD domains and Korean topic labels were reviewed per question; the review form lets learners override a suggested domain. LIVE's numeric difficulty rating remains source metadata, not a GFIELD grade, rank, percentile, or calibrated difficulty band. No original question, answer, hint, or solution text is copied.

`amc8-item-concept-source-inventory.csv` covers every AMC 8 paper currently listed by LIVE: 41 years from 1985–2026 (2021 had no AMC 8), 25 questions per paper, 1,025 rows. Each row contains published source concept tags, the site's numeric difficulty rating, item URL, and year solution URL. `amc8-gfield-concept-crosswalk.csv` maps all 175 unique LIVE tags to GFIELD's five broad review domains and Korean topic names. The 2010 paper uses question-level reviewed overrides; other years use editable domain suggestions derived from the source-tag crosswalk. This crosswalk does not independently establish item difficulty, grade placement, or mastery. The diagnosis remains based on a learner's recorded answers; source ratings are not used as scores or levels.

For the legacy AMC 8 inventory, `python3 boarding-school-math/scripts/build_amc8_item_concept_reference.py` is retained as a compatibility entry point to the complete AMC/AIME builder. Use the full-process commands below to refresh all contests; the build step reads checked-in metadata and does not download or copy exam content.

`../amc-original-source-catalog.csv` indexes all 266 archived AMC 8/10/12 and AIME year/forms, including original printable PDF, answer-key, and solution URLs. `../amc-item-concept-source-inventory.csv` now records 6,500 question-level metadata rows across those 266 forms: source concept tags, published difficulty where available, and direct problem/solution URLs. The inventory contains no question statements, choices, answer keys, hints, or solution prose. AMC 8 rows retain the earlier item inventory. AMC 10, AMC 12, and AIME rows were fetched from each LIVE solution page.

`../amc-live-concept-taxonomy.csv` snapshots 256 named LIVE concept-index entries and their source taxonomy groups. `../amc-gfield-concept-crosswalk-all.csv` maps all 911 unique observed source tags to six editable GFIELD review domains. Tags found in the current LIVE taxonomy use that source group as evidence; 655 legacy/alternate source tags absent from the current index use an automatic slug-based suggestion and are explicitly marked for teacher review. The item-level domain is a proposed broad diagnostic category, not a calibrated skill-level or validated item difficulty. Learners can change it on the answer-entry screen. `../amc-original-item-concept-reference.js` exposes all 6,500 references to AMC/AIME paper review.

Refresh the public-source snapshots with `python3 boarding-school-math/scripts/fetch_live_amc_item_inventory.py`, then rebuild the browser reference and full crosswalk with `python3 boarding-school-math/scripts/build_amc_item_concept_reference.py`. Fetching reads LIVE's published question tags and source ratings; it does not store problem or solution text. LIVE states that its archive materials are used with official legal permission of MAA. Original printable exams, answer keys, and worked solutions remain linked to their host.
