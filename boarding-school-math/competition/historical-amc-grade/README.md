# Historical AMC grade-level results — source staging

This folder contains historical, source-attributed aggregate data. The MAA-report grade means are connected to the AMC paper review as a narrowly matched historical reference: the paper year, contest, and an exact grade must all match. The reference appears only after every answer has been marked. It is labeled as a past cohort mean, never as a current norm, student level, percentile, or placement result. Korea-only score distributions remain research data and are not mixed into the national historical means.

`amc8-2011-korea-grade-percentile.csv` transcribes the MAA report table reproduced by AMC Study for the 2011 AMC 8 Korea participant cohort (899 reported participants). It contains only score-by-grade-group counts and the table's reported percentile values; no names, school records, or individual results are copied.

For a completed 2011 AMC 8 attempt, the paper-review screen can show a matching row from this table (grades 6–8 exact; grades 1–5 map to the report's combined `<=5` bucket). It displays the source's percentile value as historical Korea-cohort context, labels the cohort and bucket, and links back to the transcription. Rows without a reported percentile remain hidden. For a completed 2009 AMC 8 attempt, it can calculate the share of the same-grade Korea cohort scoring strictly below the entered score from the complete score-frequency table in `amc8-2009-korea-grade-score-count.csv`. The screen labels that calculated method and links to its source; it does not call it an MAA-reported percentile. Neither comparison claims a current percentile, rank, placement, award, or US/international norm. `amc8-korea-percentile-reference.js` provides the screen lookup while the CSVs remain the transcription record of truth.

`amc8-korea-grade-means-2009-2011.csv` records the grade-level mean and participant counts printed in the 2009 and 2011 Korea AMC 8 result pages. These are Korea-reported cohorts, not US or global norms, and remain separate from MAA report-cohort averages. The 2009 listed grade counts total 737 of 741; the 2011 listed grade groups total 894 of 899. Keep those unaccounted-for grade records visible when using the table.

`amc8-2009-korea-grade-score-count.csv` transcribes the score-by-grade frequency table reproduced for the 2009 AMC 8 Korea participant cohort (741 reported participants). It has grade buckets 5–8 only and no percentile values. The four grade totals sum to 737, leaving four participants with no grade recorded in the report summary.

`amc8-2019-global-grade-score-counts.csv` records the complete score 0–25 frequency table for the 2019 AMC 8 MAA Overall grade-report image reproduced in a Beijing XDF results article. Apple Vision OCR was checked against both images. The file includes the report's unknown-grade group so all five group totals sum to 94,620. Weighted means round to the summary's reported values: ≤5, 8.48 (n=4,396); Grade 6, 8.50 (n=18,245); Grade 7, 9.35 (n=30,327); Grade 8, 10.06 (n=40,167); unknown grade, 8.09 (n=1,485). The ≤5 bucket is retained as such; it is not split into individual grades, and the unknown-grade group is not used for learner comparison. The review screen derives a strictly-lower-score share, not an official percentile, present-day norm, or ability band. The article reports that its images are MAA's 2019 score reports; the image host is a secondary reproduction, not MAA.

`amc8-2010-item-response-percent.csv` records the 2010 AMC 8 answer-choice percentages, omissions, unreadable responses, and correct-response rates from a scanned table attributed to MAA and reproduced by AMC Study. The post reports 153,211 students. The image was transcribed with Apple Vision and visually checked against the scan; correct answers were cross-checked against the LIVE answer key. The same file now includes the complete 2009 and 2011 answer-choice tables from AMC Study's MAA-result reproductions: 741 and 899 reported South Korea participants, respectively. For 2009 question 12, LIVE lists D as the answer while the reproduced response table marks D and E as correct; the app keeps D as the answer key and records their combined response rate (65.18%). These are item-response references for their recorded cohorts, not grade-level data, current norms, or student ability ratings. AMC Study's red prediction marks and self-reported prediction-hit claims are excluded. They appear only beside missed/unanswered items for matching AMC 8 years. The source host is a secondary reproduction, not the MAA host.

`amc8-award-cutoffs-2003-2022.csv` holds published AMC 8 whole-cohort Honor Roll (top 5%) and Distinguished Honor Roll (top 1%) minimum scores from the historical-results table for 2003–2020 and 2022. The paper-review screen shows the same-year reference after a complete manual score entry. This is a whole-cohort award cutoff, not a grade-level percentile or actual award decision. No 2021 row is added because there was no 2021 AMC 8 contest; 2023 onward uses the existing MAA EduView score-distribution reference. The old table is a secondary transcription attributed to MAA historical reports; cutoff semantics are preserved as the listed minimum score. Cross-checks include the Think Matrix table for 2013–2023 and the archived 2018 contest score report. The 2012/2013 boundary can appear as `>17` in one source, equivalent to a minimum qualifying score of 18; the staged file uses 18.

`amc8-grade-means-2001-2009.csv` stages recorded-grade participant counts and mean scores for AMC 8 years 2001–2009. The 2001–2002 cells come from an MAA-authored 2008 results report; the 2003–2009 cells come from an MAA-authored 2009 report. Both copies are hosted by third parties and still need primary-host reconciliation. Grade groups are 4–5, 6, 7, and 8.

`amc10-12-grade-means-2007-2011.csv` stages 110 historical AMC 10/12 records from MAA-authored national statistical reports: grade-group mean and participant count, separated by contest, year, and A/B form. The 2007–2010 values are from the 2010 annual report; 2011 values are from a copy of the posted grand-statistics report. Grade groups preserve the report wording (`8 and below` vs exact `8`, and `unknown`). These are historical descriptive means, not percentile ranks, cut scores, or level labels.

- `amc-grade-reference-coverage-by-paper.csv` maps all 266 year/form entries in the original-paper catalog to the current grade-mean evidence status. `historical-grade-mean-staged` means a report copy with grade means/counts was staged; `no-grade-table-recovered` means this research pass did not recover a complete national grade-mean table and does not prove none exists; `aime-grade-mean-not-yet-inventoried` means AIME mean coverage is outside this dataset. The manifest also carries original-paper routes, exact grade groups, grouped grade groups, and report-copy URLs.
- `amc8-2009-global-grade-score-counts.csv` records a complete 2009 MAA all-reporting-schools score-by-grade table transcribed from a contemporaneous copy and reconciled against the MAA annual report's recorded-grade sample counts and grade means. Grade 4–5 remains a combined bucket. The review UI derives only the share with a strictly lower score; it does not call this value an official percentile or norm.
- Exact-grade historical mean coverage: AMC 8 2001–2009 (one contest per year; grade buckets 4–5, 6, 7, 8); AMC 10 and AMC 12 2007–2011 A/B (AMC 10 buckets 8 and below, 9, 10; AMC 12 has exact grades 8–13 where reported). Grouped buckets do not match an individual grade in the review screen. Missing year/form/grade means the comparison stays hidden.
- `amc10-12-aime-qualification-cutoffs-2000-2025.csv` stages AMC 10/12 AIME qualification score lines by year and form (2000–2001 single form; 2021 spring A/B and fall A/B). The result panel compares only the selected same-year/form attempt score to its historically published line; it is not an official invitation or a grade-level comparison. The 2024 AMC 12A entry is the revised 76.5 line, not the initially published 85.5. The 2025 values are secondary transcriptions cross-checked with MAA's official threshold announcement; the MAA page embeds the values as a remote image that was not accessible to Apple's Vision OCR from this environment. Older years are secondary historical transcriptions. Treat these as historical references, not current qualification rules. The 2026 competition uses fixed 100/85 score requirements and is not an observed historical cutoff row in this file.
- The 2011 AMC 8 Korea percentile table and 2009 Korea score-by-grade counts cover Korea-reported cohorts only. Each is shown only with its matching AMC 8 year and grade. The 2011 source percentile is copied from that year's table; the 2009 below-score share is explicitly calculated from the complete score-frequency table. Neither is a learner-specific standing or a percentile for another population/year.
- Rows with a blank percentile correspond to source cells that had no numeric value because no participant was listed for that score/group.
- Source omitted score 1 because no score-1 row was present; it is deliberately not synthesized here.

`amc8-2013-school-grade-item-response.csv` transcribes the Grade 7 (n=63) and Grade 8 (n=55) answer-choice tables from a 2013 MAA-generated Mathematics Ed Clinic school report reproduced by Asia Maths Alliance. The review screen uses a row only when AMC 8 year 2013 and the selected grade (7 or 8) match. It labels the school cohort and links the report. These small, single-school groups are not national grade norms or achievement levels. Other grades remain without a response comparison.

`amc8-2015-school-grade7-item-response.csv` records the 25 answer-choice rows for Grade 7 (n=10) from a 2015 MAA-generated report for one Singapore school. The review screen matches only AMC 8 2015 Grade 7. This is a small school cohort, not a national grade norm; it must not be pooled with other schools or years. The source report also lists school and student-level information; only the aggregate grade response table is transcribed here.
