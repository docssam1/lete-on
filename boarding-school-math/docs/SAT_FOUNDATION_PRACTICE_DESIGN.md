# GFIELD SAT Math foundation practice · design note

## Need and audience

- **Learning need:** Grade 6–10 learners need an optional way to revisit foundational rates, percentages, algebraic relationships, geometry, and data interpretation before choosing broader official SAT practice.
- **Audience and motivation:** Optional, self-selected review for Grades 6–10 whose annual plans are supplied or explicitly labeled as inferred. Grades 6–7 use it as a bridge; Grades 8–10 can revisit foundational ideas before official SAT-format practice. Advanced Algebra items are explicitly marked as optional extension.
- **Delivery:** Standalone, responsive GFIELD page linked from the Grade 6 and Grade 7 roadmap stages; no LMS tracking or server-side learner records.

## Observable outcomes

After practice, a learner can:

1. choose and calculate a unit rate or proportional amount with units;
2. represent a percent or linear situation with an equation and explain the meaning of its terms;
3. read a small data set and calculate a measure of center;
4. distinguish what a voluntary sample can and cannot support.
5. simplify an equivalent expression, evaluate a nonlinear function, and recognize a simple exponential pattern;
6. apply selected angle, circle, area, and volume relationships.

## Scope and evidence

- Twenty original GFIELD questions sample selected skills across all four College Board SAT Math domains: Algebra, Advanced Math, Problem-Solving and Data Analysis, and Geometry and Trigonometry. Four Advanced Math questions are marked as optional extension. The set is a foundation review for selected grade routes, not a miniature SAT or a complete SAT syllabus.
- Official domain names and sample skill categories are linked to College Board's content-domain and Student Question Bank pages. No official questions are copied.
- Feedback displays hints, worked reasoning, and a transfer question. Correct-answer count is only local session progress; it does not report a score, proficiency, readiness, or placement.
- Evaluate the prototype through answer-key review, keyboard/accessibility checks, responsive browser review, and direct link tests. Learner pilot feedback remains a later step.
